The Coast Path

The coast path starts at the harbour wall and climbs quickly onto the cliffs. For the first kilometre the sea is on your left and sheep fields on your right.

After the old coastguard station the path dips into a wooded valley. The stream at the bottom is easy to cross in summer, but after rain it is worth using the footbridge a little upstream.

The climb out of the valley is the hardest part of the day. Take it slowly: there is a bench at the top with the best view on the whole route.

Check the tide times before you set off. Parts of the beach route are cut off at high tide.

Wear boots with a good grip. The cliff path is muddy after rain.

Keep dogs on a lead near the cliff edge and in fields with sheep.

Mobile signal is poor between the valley and the lighthouse.

Tell someone your route and when you expect to be back.

From the bench the path is level for three kilometres. Look out for seals on the rocks below the lighthouse, especially at low tide.

At the lighthouse you can either turn inland back to the village or carry on to the next cove, which adds about an hour.

If you carry on, the path narrows and runs close to the edge. It is safe in good weather but not a place to be in strong wind.

The cove has a small café that opens at weekends from Easter to October. In winter, bring a flask.

The return route follows a farm track and then a quiet lane. The last kilometre is downhill all the way to the harbour.
