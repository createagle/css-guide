A float takes an element out of the normal line flow and pushes it to one side. Lines of text then shorten to make room for it. By default, text wraps around the float's margin box, which is always a rectangle, even when the image inside looks round.

The shape-outside property changes that. It gives the float a different wrapping shape: a circle, an ellipse, a polygon, an inset rectangle with rounded corners, or the transparent parts of an image. The float itself does not change size or shape; only the lines around it do.

Use shape-margin to keep the text away from the edge of the shape. Without it, the letters touch the curve. This is a small detail, but magazines have done it for a long time and it makes a page feel designed.
